## Delivery

Speak like the founder showing one surprising interaction to a judge. Read the opening quote with quiet confidence, then pause before “Let’s put it to the test.” Give “Fewer ships. Less income.” a deliberate rhythm. Emphasize **could** in the teacher’s question and **possible, not inevitable** in the revision. Let the closing line breathe. Avoid a trailer voice or constant excitement.

Keep the voice as one connected performance. Aim for roughly 145–155 spoken words per minute; final duration must be checked from the actual recording. Remove waiting and setup from the screen take instead of rushing the voice. Do not freeze the screen merely to fill a slot.

## What earns the judges’ attention

- **Immediate stakes:** the opening gives viewers a claim to question, before naming the product.
- **Visible Astra contribution:** the model challenges an argument and generates the teacher-requested question; the teacher previews and applies it.
- **Human control:** hold long enough on preview → apply for the teacher’s role to register.
- **A concrete payoff:** end on the revised reasoning, rather than making a rubric score the headline.
- **Development contribution:** the closing proof line notes that the product was built with Astra. If judges ask, use the build log to describe actual implementation work; do not invent a percentage or time-saving metric.

All seven scenes use the verified prepared Alexandria workflow. Retain on-screen source/assumption labels and the AI-narration/edited-waits disclosure. The opening is a deliberate cold open; the teacher scene starts the walkthrough afterward. The existing source recordings can support a new edit, but the new voice, captions, and cut timing must be generated together before calling the video version 3.

### Initial student claim

The library would definitely close because fewer ships means no knowledge.

Actual first response, 1/4:

> Your prediction is clear, but fewer ships do not mean knowledge disappears. We must distinguish a library building from its scholarly community. In this hypothetical scenario, reduced support depends on assumed trade-linked funding; closure is not certain.

Actual follow-up: “Could a new patron sustain the scholars despite fewer ships?”

### Teacher intervention — standard request

At the harbor, show a hypothetical ledger comparing ship arrivals. In simple language, ask whether a new patron could support the scholars even if trade falls. Help this preview learner question certainty without giving the answer.

The first version-2 preview was **Fewer ships—less support?**. The recorded pickup preview was **Fewer ships, fewer options?**. Its actual guiding question:
