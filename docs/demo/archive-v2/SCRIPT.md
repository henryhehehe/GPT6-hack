## Copy-and-paste inputs

### Initial student claim

The library would definitely close because fewer ships means no knowledge.

Actual first response, 1/4:

> Your prediction is clear, but fewer ships do not mean knowledge disappears. We must distinguish a library building from its scholarly community. In this hypothetical scenario, reduced support depends on assumed trade-linked funding; closure is not certain.

Actual follow-up: “Could a new patron sustain the scholars despite fewer ships?”

### Teacher intervention — standard request

At the harbor, show a hypothetical ledger comparing ship arrivals. In simple language, ask whether a new patron could support the scholars even if trade falls. Help this preview learner question certainty without giving the answer.

The first version-2 preview was **Fewer ships—less support?**. The recorded pickup preview was **Fewer ships, fewer options?**. Its actual guiding question:

> If trade falls, could a new patron still pay to support the scholars—and what would that mean for saying the library must close?

### Revised student explanation

I revise my claim: closure is possible, not inevitable. The invented harbor ledger changes from 12 ships to 4; fewer arrivals could reduce goods, merchant income, and trade-linked support for scholars. That funding link is an exercise assumption. Strabo describes a learned community in the royal palaces, not trade as its only funding source [Strabo, Geography 17.1.8]. A new patron could replace lost support. The teacher's question made me separate a plausible mechanism from a certain outcome.

Actual revised response, 4/4:

> Your revision is careful: you distinguish the invented ledger from Strabo’s account and an assumed funding pathway from inevitable closure. Strabo supports a scholarly community within the royal palaces, not exclusive dependence on trade. Remember, the community’s survival and a building’s survival are different questions.
