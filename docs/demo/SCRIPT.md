## Voiceover

“The library will definitely close.” That's our student's first answer. Let's put it to the test.

Counterfactual Worlds turns history and literature into places students can explore.

Teachers start with thirty prepared lessons, review their sources, and bring real museum objects into the classroom.

In Alexandria, collapse harbor trade. Explore the city. Compare what changes, and what we're only assuming.

Read Strabo in context. Save evidence. Build an argument. Astra challenges claims that go beyond the sources.

The teacher adds a new question: could a replacement patron support the scholars? The student keeps their work and revises.

Now: “Closure is possible, not inevitable.” Teachers see the first and latest explanations together.

Our goal: richer learning, whatever the budget.

## Exact rehearsal inputs

Create “Could knowledge survive a trade collapse?” from the first Alexandria catalog lesson, review its source packet, and launch. Use the teacher’s practice learner. Add the Arsinoe II coin from Museum objects.

First answer:

> The library will definitely close because fewer ships means no knowledge.

Teacher request:

> Ask whether a replacement patron could support the scholars. Help this learner revise the certainty in their first answer. Keep the question short, distinguish Strabo from our invented ledger and funding assumption, and do not give a full model answer.

Select the practice learner with the persisted first answer. Use **Use standard request**, inspect the preview, then share with the class. The actual preview was “Test the funding link”:

> Could a replacement patron support the scholars, and how would that change the certainty of your first claim?

Revised answer:

> Closure is possible, not inevitable. In the invented harbor ledger, arrivals fall from 12 ships to 4. Fewer ships could reduce merchant income and then scholar support IF that support depends on trade-linked revenue. That funding link is an exercise assumption. Strabo describes shared facilities, property and appointed leadership at the Mouseion [Strabo, Geography 17.1.8 · H. L. Jones translation (1932)]. He does not establish a trade-funded budget or the fate of the Library's collections. A replacement patron could support the scholars. The teacher's question changed my claim from certainty to a conditional explanation.

## Actual results and boundaries

The initial answer received provisional model feedback of 0/4; the revision received 4/4. The report contains two real submissions by **Your practice learner**, zero joined learners, and **Context changed**. These are demonstration rubric results, not measured educational impact. Exact submissions and feedback remain in `output/demo/v5/learning-report.html`.

The teacher request used the standard request path, informed by the selected practice learner’s existing answer, then shared a classwide question. It is not native in-flight steering. The museum record supplies discussion context; argument feedback still uses assigned source cards. The invented ledger and funding assumption must remain distinct from Strabo’s evidence.

Pictures are real recordings; generation waits are edited out. Quote overlays are labeled actual excerpts. Narration uses OpenAI Marin speech; Astra powers the demonstrated app reasoning. “Richer learning, whatever the budget” states the founder’s goal. The printable kit demonstrates a concrete paper option, not proven learning gains or universal offline device support.
