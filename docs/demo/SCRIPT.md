## Voiceover

“The library will definitely close.” That’s the first answer. Let’s test it.

Counterfactual Worlds turns history and literature into investigations students can walk through.

Teachers choose from thirty prepared lessons, review the sources, and get a teaching guide.

In Alexandria, we collapse harbor trade. Fewer ships. Less income. But must the scholars disappear?

Students read Strabo, cite the evidence, and defend their explanation. Astra challenges unsupported certainty.

Then the teacher changes the challenge: could a new patron help? Preview. Apply. The student keeps their work.

Now the answer changes: “Closure is possible, not inevitable.” The teacher can review the first and latest explanations side by side.

Built with Astra. Counterfactual Worlds: step inside a question. Come back with an argument.

## Exact rehearsal inputs

Create the first Alexandria catalog lesson, “Could knowledge survive a trade collapse?” Review and approve its source packet, then launch. Join as a separate learner called Demo Learner.

First answer:

> The library will definitely close because fewer ships means no knowledge.

Teacher request:

> A student claims the library will definitely close when harbor trade collapses. Ask whether a new patron could support the scholars. Keep the question short, distinguish Strabo from the invented ledger, and help students revise certainty without giving away a complete answer.

Use the standard request path. Review the actual preview, then apply it to the student world. This recording produced “Test the funding link” and the question:

> Could a new patron support the scholars, and how would that change your “definitely closes” claim?

Revised explanation:

> Closure is possible, not inevitable. In the invented harbor ledger, arrivals fall from 12 ships to 4. Fewer arrivals could reduce goods and merchant income, then scholar support IF that support depends on trade-linked revenue. This funding link is an exercise assumption. Strabo describes shared facilities, property, and appointed leadership at the Mouseion [Strabo, Geography 17.1.8 · H. L. Jones translation (1932)]. He does not establish a trade-funded budget or the fate of the Library's collections. A new patron could replace lost support. The teacher's question changed my claim from certainty to a conditional explanation.

## Actual results and claim boundaries

The first answer received 1/4; the revision received 4/4. These are model rubric results from this demonstration, not a measured learning outcome. The teacher report correctly marks “Context changed” because applying the teacher question changed the world version. Keep that label visible. The film emphasizes the submitted explanations, not a score increase.

The app reasoning uses Astra. Narration uses OpenAI speech generation with Marin. The teacher challenge used a standard request and was applied classwide; do not describe it as native in-flight steering or selected-learner personalization. The Strabo reading and the invented 12-to-4 ledger have different provenance. Keep the funding dependence conditional.

All film pictures are actual moving app recordings. Quotes are labeled editorial excerpts. Generation waits are cut; the minute is not an elapsed-latency claim. A few low-contrast app labels remain visible in this build, so excerpts provide readable emphasis without replacing the interface.
