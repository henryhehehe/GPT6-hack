## Copy-and-paste inputs

### Initial student claim

The library would definitely close because fewer ships means no knowledge.

Actual first response, 1/4:

> I’d distinguish closing a building from losing a scholarly community. Your prediction is clear, but fewer ships do not mean knowledge disappears. Trade-dependent support is a scenario assumption; Strabo does not establish trade as the only funding source.

Actual follow-up: “Could a new patron sustain the scholars despite fewer ships?”

### Teacher intervention — standard request

At the harbor, show a hypothetical ledger comparing ship arrivals. In simple language, ask whether a new patron could support the scholars even if trade falls. Help this preview learner question certainty without giving the answer.

Actual preview title: **Fewer ships—what about support?** Actual guiding question:

> If trade falls, could a new patron still pay to support the scholars—and what would that mean for your claim that the library would definitely close?

Click **Add to student world** only after reviewing the actual preview. The exercise counts are invented, not ancient records. The current preview describes a visual teaching prop in prose; do not promise that every object mentioned in that prose is dynamically rendered as geometry.

### Evidence to save

- **The harbor ledger** (`ledger`): invented 12 → 4 ship counts; a teaching prop.
- **Who pays for knowledge?** (`funding`): explicit trade-linked funding assumption and possible replacement patron.
- **A community of scholars** (`strabo`): source/reading note; open the reader and use its citation action.

### Revised student explanation

I revise my claim: closure is possible, not inevitable. The invented harbor ledger changes from 12 ships to 4; fewer arrivals could reduce goods, merchant income, and trade-linked support for scholars. That funding link is an exercise assumption. Strabo describes a learned community in the royal palaces, not trade as its only funding source [Strabo, Geography 17.1.8]. A new patron could replace lost support. The teacher's question made me separate a plausible mechanism from a certain outcome.

Actual revised response, 4/4:

> Your revision carefully separates possibility from certainty. You label the ledger as invented and the funding link as assumed. Strabo supports the existence of a scholarly institution, not exclusive dependence on trade. Keep distinguishing the scholars’ survival from the building’s fate.
