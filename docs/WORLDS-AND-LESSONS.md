## Shared teaching contract

Students predict, examine sources, select passages and explain their relevance, defend a claim, receive a targeted teacher challenge, then revise and explain what changed. Reading and evidence must remain accessible without walking. Scene art, generated dialogue, and invented branches are labeled separately from source text.

History uses claim, evidence/provenance, inquiry-appropriate reasoning, and a limitation or alternative. Literature uses interpretive claim, textual evidence, analysis, and an alternative reading. Different supported conclusions can satisfy the rubric. Feedback is formative; a score, visited station, or open door is not proof of learning.

Suggested 45-minute rhythm: prediction 4 min; three source stops 15; initial argument 8; challenge 5; revision 9; reflection 4. Teachers can use a text-only journal for learning artifacts while the product’s prediction, selected-citation, and before/after features remain incomplete.

## Lesson proposals

### 1. Alexandria: the systems that sustain knowledge

**Why this world:** Existing setting makes this the first place to exercise the complete reasoning loop.

**Source:** [Strabo, Geography 17.1.8](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/17A1*.html). H. L. Jones English translation on LacusCurtius; existing app card is a paraphrase. Source check: September 10, 2026; selected packet prepared locally, teacher review required.

**Reading boundary:** 17.1.8 plus separately labeled local funding assumption and invented ledger.

**Setting:** existing Alexandria exterior. Harbor → Waterfront market → House of knowledge. Every setting addition is proposed interpretive staging.

#### alexandria-01 · Could knowledge survive a trade collapse?

**Inquiry:** Under which assumptions would less trade reduce scholar support?

**Assigned selection:** 17.1.8 + local funding and ledger cards. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Classify source, assumption, and prop; draw a conditional chain with a citation at each supported step.

**Teacher challenge:** Which link does Strabo establish, and which link belongs only to this scenario?

**Revision artifact:** Rewrite one inevitable outcome as a conditional claim and identify the missing evidence.

Mode: counterfactual · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### alexandria-02 · The replacement patron

**Inquiry:** Could another source of support change the explanation?

**Assigned selection:** Same approved packet; patron replacement is an explicit scenario assumption. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Compare two funding diagrams while holding the disruption constant.

**Teacher challenge:** What would you need to observe to distinguish the two explanations?

**Revision artifact:** Add a competing mechanism and one observation that could discriminate between them.

Mode: counterfactual · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### alexandria-03 · A building or a community?

**Inquiry:** What can a description of an institution tell us about sustaining knowledge?

**Assigned selection:** 17.1.8; existing card linked to original source. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Separate statements about facilities, people, and institutional support from visual guesses.

**Teacher challenge:** Does a detailed building model add documentary evidence?

**Revision artifact:** Refine the claim about preservation and state what this source cannot answer.

Mode: source-investigation · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

**Boundaries:** Strabo does not prove the proposed trade-to-funding mechanism. The 12-to-4 ship ledger is invented. No single-event destruction story or exact architectural reconstruction is established. The Mouseion (Museum) described by Strabo is a scholarly institution; it is not interchangeable with a Library building or book collection. See [historical accuracy review](curriculum/HISTORICAL-ACCURACY-REVIEW.md) for sources and teaching context.

### 2. The Odyssey: cleverness, pride, and leadership

**Why this world:** Explicit first literature priority in LITERATURE-EXPANSION.md.

**Source:** [Homer, The Odyssey](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1727). Samuel Butler prose translation; use Ulysses in displayed excerpts. Source check: September 10, 2026; selected packet prepared locally, teacher review required.

**Reading boundary:** Book IX only; no later-book events, preface theories, or footnotes in model context.

**Setting:** coast prototype; authored cave and ship later. Shore briefing → Cave evidence station → Departing ship. Every setting addition is proposed interpretive staging.

#### odyssey-ix-01 · The clever escape

**Inquiry:** How does the account invite admiration for cleverness?

**Assigned selection:** Book IX: cave encounter, assumed name, and escape. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Annotate a decision and the narrator’s wording; distinguish action from self-presentation.

**Teacher challenge:** Is your evidence about what he does, or how he tells it?

**Revision artifact:** Qualify the interpretation with one detail that complicates admiration.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### odyssey-ix-02 · The name shouted back

**Inquiry:** How does revealing an identity change a reading of leadership?

**Assigned selection:** Book IX: narrated stratagem evaluation, crew warning, and shouted identity. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Compare the narrator’s claim of cleverness with his response to the crew’s warning; cite wording from both.

**Teacher challenge:** What does the crew’s objection make harder to explain?

**Revision artifact:** Revise the leadership claim without reducing the episode to a single trait.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### odyssey-ix-03 · If the name stayed hidden

**Inquiry:** What can a textual counterfactual reveal about the original choice?

**Assigned selection:** Book IX: same departure packet only. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Write a two-column comparison of textual evidence and invented consequences.

**Teacher challenge:** Which consequences are supported possibilities, and which are inventions?

**Revision artifact:** Return to the original text and explain how the comparison changes your reading.

Mode: counterfactual · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

**Boundaries:** Narrated choices are not a neutral historical record. Mythic geography and staging are interpretations. Violence stays in teacher-reviewed text; no combat reenactment.

### 3. Pride and Prejudice: revising a judgment

**Why this world:** Explicit second literature priority; makes evidence-based revision the lesson’s subject.

**Source:** [Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1342). Project Gutenberg #1342; preserve selected edition and chapter numbering. Source check: September 10, 2026; selected packet prepared locally, teacher review required.

**Reading boundary:** Chapters 35–36 only; earlier material requires a separate teacher-approved packet; nothing after 36.

**Setting:** garden prototype; authored Hunsford kit later. Hunsford lane → Letter-reading place → Comparison desk. Every setting addition is proposed interpretive staging.

#### austen-letter-01 · A letter is a claim

**Inquiry:** How should a reader evaluate a persuasive private letter?

**Assigned selection:** Chapter 35: opening and selected explanations. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Tag assertions, offered support, and appeals to the recipient.

**Teacher challenge:** What could this writer have an interest in emphasizing?

**Revision artifact:** Replace automatic acceptance or rejection with a passage-supported assessment.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### austen-letter-02 · Reading again

**Inquiry:** How does the narration represent Elizabeth’s reconsideration?

**Assigned selection:** Chapter 36: first reading, rereading, and self-assessment; no direct Chapter 35 letter passage in this packet. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Compare the narration of Elizabeth’s first reading, rereading, and self-assessment; distinguish her changing interpretation from independent proof.

**Teacher challenge:** Which phrase supports your account of the change, rather than just naming it?

**Revision artifact:** Explain a change in interpretation through wording and acknowledge an alternative reading.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### austen-letter-03 · How far should judgment change?

**Inquiry:** What remains uncertain after rereading?

**Assigned selection:** Chapters 35–36; no later corroborating events. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Build a claim/support/remaining-question table using only assigned passages.

**Teacher challenge:** Which conclusion goes beyond what this packet allows you to know?

**Revision artifact:** Narrow one overconfident conclusion and identify evidence that could change it.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

**Boundaries:** Darcy’s assertions are not independent corroboration. Elizabeth’s reassessment is arguable, not a required answer. Hunsford is not Pemberley; the room and desk are teaching staging.

### 4. Macbeth: persuasion and responsibility

**Why this world:** A compact comparison of multiple speakers supports close reading and defensible disagreement.

**Source:** [William Shakespeare, Macbeth](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1533). Project Gutenberg #1533; act/scene locators until edition-specific lines are checked. Source check: September 10, 2026; selected packet prepared locally, teacher review required.

**Reading boundary:** Act 1 scenes 3, 5, and 7; no later outcome in tutor context.

**Setting:** archive prototype; castle threshold kit proposed. Prophecy station → Letter desk → Decision threshold. Every setting addition is proposed interpretive staging.

#### macbeth-01 · Hearing a prophecy

**Inquiry:** How does Macbeth interpret what he hears?

**Assigned selection:** 1.3. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Separate the reported prediction from the listener’s inference.

**Teacher challenge:** Which words belong to the speakers, and which belong to his response?

**Revision artifact:** Revise a fate claim to account for interpretive choice.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### macbeth-02 · Pressure in language

**Inquiry:** How does persuasion work through appeals to identity?

**Assigned selection:** 1.5 and 1.7. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Compare two appeals and explain a specific rhetorical choice.

**Teacher challenge:** Does forceful language prove that persuasion succeeded?

**Revision artifact:** Distinguish rhetorical strategy from an inferred effect.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### macbeth-03 · Reasons at the threshold

**Inquiry:** How does a soliloquy complicate responsibility?

**Assigned selection:** 1.7. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Pair an expressed objection with the subsequent decision language.

**Teacher challenge:** Can your interpretation account for the strongest objection?

**Revision artifact:** Defend a reading that addresses both agency and pressure.

Mode: close-reading · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

**Boundaries:** Supernatural staging must not assert that destiny removes responsibility. Planned violence is discussed through the text, not a game action.

### 8. Philadelphia: the case for independence

**Why this world:** Document structure provides three meaningful stations without inventing eyewitness testimony.

**Source:** [Declaration of Independence transcription](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript). National Archives transcription of the adopted Declaration. Source check: September 10, 2026; selected packet prepared locally, teacher review required.

**Reading boundary:** Approved selections from principles, grievances, and conclusion; contextual claims need additional sources.

**Setting:** archive prototype; document workshop proposed. Principles desk → Grievance wall → Declaration table. Every setting addition is proposed interpretive staging.

#### declaration-01 · Principles and conclusions

**Inquiry:** How does the document connect its principles to political action?

**Assigned selection:** Opening, principles, and conclusion selections. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Map a premise and conclusion to their exact textual support.

**Teacher challenge:** Does the passage state a principle or describe existing practice?

**Revision artifact:** Separate the document’s argument from a claim about lived conditions.

Mode: source-investigation · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### declaration-02 · What a grievance can prove

**Inquiry:** How do accumulated grievances support persuasion?

**Assigned selection:** Teacher-selected grievance paragraphs. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Group allegations and identify the external evidence needed to test one.

**Teacher challenge:** Is repetition corroboration?

**Revision artifact:** Qualify one historical claim by naming the source’s purpose and limits.

Mode: source-investigation · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

#### declaration-03 · The declaration as an action

**Inquiry:** What changes when an argument becomes a collective declaration?

**Assigned selection:** Conclusion with approved opening comparison. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

**Evidence task:** Compare descriptive, justificatory, and declarative language.

**Teacher challenge:** Does announcing an outcome prove that it has been secured?

**Revision artifact:** Distinguish an asserted political act from its unverified consequences.

Mode: source-investigation · Draft · 45-minute planning estimate.

**Boundaries:** A grievance is an allegation in an advocacy document, not independently verified by its presence. Universal language does not establish universal participation or rights in practice. Distinguish the independence vote (July 2, 1776), adoption (July 4), and most signatures on the engrossed copy (August 2). See [historical accuracy review](curriculum/HISTORICAL-ACCURACY-REVIEW.md) for sources and teaching context.

#### douglass-literacy-03 · Knowledge and its costs

**Inquiry:** How does testimony complicate a simple story of progress?

**Assigned selection:** Chapter VII: selected learning, argument, and distress passages. Exact excerpts must be approved before use.

## Before a draft becomes a usable pack

1. Save the exact approved passages, edition/translator, source URL, locator, retrieval date, notices, and a content hash. Verify quotations against the saved source. Use stable passage IDs and an explicit offset convention; do not invent page or line numbers.
2. Teacher checks range fidelity, content, reading support, and inquiry. Optional vocabulary notes and paraphrases remain visibly distinct from quotations. Confirm permissions for the actual edition and included images before redistribution.
3. Fit a reviewed packet to current contracts: three internal zones (`harbor`, `market`, `library`), three characters and activities, three to six source cards, at most 700 characters per card, and at least one local linked card at each stop. These legacy IDs are internal mappings, not license to portray every book as Alexandria.
4. Preserve omitted context in a readable approved packet. The importer’s bounded excerpt selection is not whole-book coverage. The full source URL can contain spoilers; student “read in context” should open the approved range, with out-of-range access controlled by the teacher.
5. Exercise a fresh classroom, selected citations, out-of-range rejection, a supported disagreement, a fabricated-quote rejection, revision persistence, and an accessible non-3D route. Complete the applicable product readiness gates before advertising classroom readiness.
