# Living reading landscapes

The world should establish a place before introducing its reading companions. Overview labels and floating diamonds have been replaced by nearby-only companion names and small ground markers. The three source/evidence roles remain available through the directory and the actual reading props.

Outdoor settings use irregular groves, branched trees, low mixed vegetation, flowers in existing garden beds, and small distant birds. Inland scenes have a deeper wooded backdrop; coastal trees remain on the land and preserve open water. Macbeth uses bare trees, muted heath cover, and outer rocks. Winter streets retain bare silhouettes. Cutaway interiors keep their architecture rather than gaining indoor meadows. Tree trunks have navigation blockers; reading aprons, arrivals, and connecting routes stay clear. Reduced-motion mode freezes birds.

## Painting references

These are visual composition references, not historical evidence about the books or modeled locations. No painting files are bundled or presented as documentary reconstructions.

- John Constable, *Wivenhoe Park, Essex* (1816), [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/1147-wivenhoe-park-essex): clustered trees, open meadow and depth beyond an estate. Applied as uneven groves and a wooded garden backdrop; the painting's pond is not reproduced.
- Claude, *Seaport with the Embarkation of the Queen of Sheba* (1648), [National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/claude-seaport-with-the-embarkation-of-the-queen-of-sheba): framing at the sides and an open water horizon. Applied to coastal planting composition, not claimed as ancient Aegean geography.
- Horatio McCulloch, *Glencoe, Argyllshire* (1864), [National Galleries of Scotland](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/6374/glencoe-argyllshire): rugged land and restrained warm accents. Applied as sparse heath vegetation and scattered outer rocks.
- J. M. W. Turner, *Calais Pier* (1803), [National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/joseph-mallord-william-turner-calais-pier): storm contrast and directional motion. The Tempest keeps its existing storm light and rough water, with leaning trees; this pass does not add rain or reproduce the painting.
