# Counterfactual Worlds: learning UX references

Research date: 10 September 2026. Scope: five relevant products, using official product pages, help articles, and published teaching materials. This is a desk review, not hands-on account testing or a verified evaluation of their current complete interfaces. Recommendations below are design inferences; marketing efficacy claims are not treated as proof that our app improves learning.

## Five patterns to borrow

| Reference | Documented behavior | Adapt for Counterfactual Worlds | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ubisoft Discovery Tour** | Freely explorable historical settings coexist with guided, expert-curated tours. Ubisoft describes short, visually attractive tours and distinguishing historical information from creative reconstruction. | Give students a full-screen setting with an optional trail of three meaningful inquiry stops. Inspecting an artifact opens its source without losing location. Explain which architecture and causal events are reconstructions. | A compulsory audio tour, a wall of encyclopedia copy, or visual realism presented as historical evidence. |
| **Minecraft Education** | Camera images enter a Portfolio; learners caption images and combine them with writing in Book & Quill. Official teaching material explicitly uses these artifacts for assessment. | A small field notebook gathers an observation, an exact source passage, and the student's explanation. An image alone is an observation, not proof. Retain an exportable final argument. | Rewarding object pickup or time spent as if it demonstrates understanding; importing Minecraft's visual style. |
| **Perusall** | Students highlight text or pin/mark image regions, then annotate in a neighboring conversation. Media comments attach to timestamps. Teacher confusion reports group student questions by topic. | Anchor evidence to specific passages/image regions; “Use in my argument” creates a citation chip linked back to the exact source. Teacher view foregrounds misconceptions and actual quotes. | A permanently open annotation sidebar in the exploration view; opaque automated grades or comment-count competition. |
| **Labster** | A Theory tab is accessible inside the simulation through the LabPad. Contextual questions appear during the activity. | Keep a source reader available on demand throughout exploration and writing. Ask the learner to predict, inspect, explain, and revise around a visible change. | Converting close reading into one correct multiple-choice answer or interrupting every movement with a modal. |
| **Mission US** | Interactive historical narratives emphasize perspective, discussion, and weighing evidence; the project explicitly does not define success by winning/losing or a game score. Teacher materials include reflection and document-based activities. | Frame a situated dilemma; reveal another perspective; conclude with a revised interpretation and reasons. In literature, allow competing defensible readings. | Implying a single “correct” literary interpretation, or treating a character's invented speech as a quotation from the novel. |

## Five critical recommendations

1. **Make student exploration the primary composition.** World occupies the viewport, with one inquiry top-left, location/proximity action near the object, and a compact notebook button. Put authoring, learner tables, model telemetry, and rubric management in the teacher workspace. While walking, show no permanent dashboard columns. On inspecting a source, open a readable focused overlay and pause movement. Preserve camera/location on close.
2. **Teach through a visible reasoning loop.** Pose a question → initial prediction → inspect and annotate two sources → defend a claim → receive one targeted challenge → revise. Display “What changed in your reasoning?” alongside the revision. Do not equate evidence count, exploration distance, or an unlocked door with mastery.
3. **Deliver the source inside the app.** Reader contains the actual excerpt, selectable text, image/zoom where relevant, full citation, passage locator, and provenance. A citation chip reopens that exact excerpt; external publisher links are secondary provenance. Do not force learners to leave the world to complete the task.
4. **Make the teacher dashboard explain learning.** Show each learner's initial/revised claim, cited passages, missing reasoning, and intervention history. Group by a concrete need such as “assertion without textual support.” Teacher can preview a targeted hint before applying it. Keep AI feedback inspectable and provisional.
5. **Distinguish text, interpretation, and invention everywhere.** Original text, student inference, teaching assumption, and generated what-if each get explicit labels. For The Odyssey, identify the translation and book/line or passage locator; for Pride and Prejudice, chapter and edition. A period painting can illustrate reception/context without documenting the novel's events. Generated scenery is labeled reconstruction; invented NPC dialogue is labeled simulation.

## Evidence reader contract

Proposed source fields: stable source ID; evidence kind; title; author/creator; edition/translator; date; passage locator; verbatim excerpt; optional image plus alt text; institution/publisher; source URL; rights/license; relation to the inquiry; source-backed versus reconstructed status. Quote selections store source version and character offsets (or another stable selector), so a revised source cannot silently invalidate an old claim. Keep the original available beside any simplified explanation. Asset selection must verify that images/text may be bundled; do not take another product's images or protected visual assets.

Student actions: **Read → select passage → explain why it matters → add to argument.** A selected source card is not evidence of comprehension until the learner uses it. Astra feedback should point to the learner's wording and cited passage, ask for a missing connection, and support alternative interpretations. Teacher review and an exported before/after artifact make the learning observable; they do not constitute a validated learning-outcome study.

## Implementation order and acceptance

**P0 — immersive shell + source reader.** A student can enter, inspect the actual source, select a passage, cite it, and return to the same camera position without opening an external tab. Keyboard-only and reduced-motion alternatives remain available. Test the full loop, not just screenshots.

**P0 — learning trace.** Persist prediction, source/version/selection, claim, feedback, revision, and teacher intervention. Teacher can explain why feedback was issued by inspecting the linked text; visiting every location alone earns no reasoning credit.

**P1 — literature pilot.** Build one complete scene and source set before introducing a pack selector with empty choices. Odyssey: contrast a successful tactic with its later consequences. Pride and Prejudice: compare an initial impression with later textual evidence. Verify passages/translation before implementation. Both ask for interpretation, not invented historical causation.

**P1 — environmental polish.** Improve scale, lighting, sound controls, proximity cues, and purposeful animations after the evidence loop works. A textured landmark adds atmosphere; meaningful action makes that atmosphere educational.

For the 60-second film, prioritize a visible **claim → cited source → teacher challenge → revised claim** sequence. Show movement and the setting as the invitation into that sequence. The compelling result is a student's reasoning visibly changing.
